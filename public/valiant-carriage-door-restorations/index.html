What A Carriage Door Restoration Includes

Surface & Finish

  • Cutting back failed paint, stain, and clear coat
  • Repairing dents, gouges, splits, and soft spots
  • Refinishing to match the existing house and trim

Structure & Hardware

  • Re-squaring sagging panels and loose joints
  • Replacing worn hinges, rollers, and carriage hardware
  • Correcting track alignment and door travel

Balance & Safety

  • Re-checking spring balance after weight changes
  • Cable tension and drum inspection
  • Opener force settings and tested safety reversal

Restore Or Replace?

Restoration Usually Wins When

The frame, stiles, and rails are structurally sound, the damage is on the surface, or the door is an architectural match to the house that would be expensive or impossible to reproduce.

Replacement Makes Sense When

There is widespread rot, the core has delaminated through, or structural rails are damaged beyond safe repair. We tell you plainly when that is the case instead of selling a finish over a failing door.

What You Get Either Way

An itemized estimate after inspection, a clear explanation of what is failing and why, and a balanced, safety-tested door when the work is finished.

Expert Answer

"Most Carriage Doors That Look Beyond Saving Are Only Failing On The Surface. The Structure Underneath Is Usually Still Good."

Valiant Garage Door restores carriage house doors across Pleasanton, Dublin, Livermore, San Ramon, Danville, Fremont, and Sunol, and we always re-balance the springs and test safety reversal after restoration work because refinishing and hardware changes alter how much the door weighs.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is carriage house garage door restoration?

Carriage door restoration is the process of saving an existing carriage house style door instead of replacing it. Valiant strips and refinishes the face, repairs or replaces damaged sections and hardware, re-squares the panels, and then re-balances the door so it runs correctly on its springs and tracks.

Can my old carriage door be restored instead of replaced?

Usually yes. If the frame and stiles are structurally sound, restoration is almost always possible even when the surface looks rough. Doors are typically only replaced when there is widespread rot, delamination through the core, or damage to the structural rails that cannot be safely repaired.

How long does carriage door restoration take?

Most carriage door restorations are staged over one to three visits depending on how much surface preparation, drying, and refinishing time the door needs. Hardware, spring, and balance work is normally completed in a single visit.

Do you restore real wood carriage doors?

Yes. We restore solid wood and wood-clad carriage doors, including overlay carriage doors on steel or composite cores. Surface preparation and refinishing are matched to the material so the finish bonds properly and lasts.

How much does carriage door restoration cost?

Restoration is quoted after inspection because the scope varies widely with door size, material, finish condition, and how much hardware or structural repair is required. You receive an itemized estimate before any work is approved.

Will restoration fix a carriage door that sags or binds?

Often yes. Sagging and binding usually trace back to loose joints, worn hinges and rollers, or a door that is out of square. Restoration addresses those causes directly, then we re-check travel and balance so the door stops dragging.

Do you re-balance the springs after a restoration?

Always. Refinishing and hardware changes can alter door weight, so we re-check spring balance, cable tension, and opener force after the work and test safety reversal before leaving.

Do you offer restoration service outside Pleasanton?

Yes. We handle carriage door restoration across Pleasanton, Dublin, Livermore, San Ramon, Danville, Fremont, Sunol, and nearby East Bay communities.
