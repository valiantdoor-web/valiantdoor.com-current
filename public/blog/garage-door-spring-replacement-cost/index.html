Garage Door Spring Replacement Cost

Spring pricing confuses people because two doors on the same street can need very different parts. This guide explains what actually moves the number so a quote makes sense when you hear it.

What You Are Actually Paying For

A spring job is three things bundled together: the spring hardware itself, the labor to release and reset tension safely, and the balancing work that makes the door operate correctly afterward.

Skip any of the three and the door comes back. That is why a spring quote is not just a parts price, and why the same part number can land at different totals on different doors.

The Cost Drivers, In Order Of Impact

DriverWhy it moves the price
Door weightHeavier doors need larger wire and longer springs, which cost more

Cycle ratingHigh-cycle springs use more steel and cost more up front

Door Weight Decides The Spring

Springs are chosen to counterbalance a specific weight. A light single-car steel door might weigh well under a hundred pounds. A wide insulated double door is much heavier, and a solid wood or carriage-style door heavier still.

Weight drives four measurements: wire diameter, inside diameter, overall length, and wind direction. Get those wrong and the door will feel heavy, drift open, or eat its opener. This is the honest reason a technician measures instead of quoting sight unseen.

Cycle Life: The Number Nobody Tells Homeowners

Springs are rated in cycles, not years. One cycle is one open plus one close. A common standard spring is rated around ten thousand cycles.

Daily cyclesRoughly how the household uses the door10,000-cycle spring lasts about
2One car, out and back once a dayWell over a decade
4Typical two-driver householdCloser to seven years
8Busy family, kids, side entrance habitAround three to four years
12 or moreRental, home business, main entry doorA couple of years

If your door runs constantly, paying more for a higher cycle rating is often the cheaper decision over ten years. If it runs twice a day, standard springs are usually right.

Single Versus Paired Springs

Most sectional doors use two torsion springs on a shared shaft. When one breaks, the other has done the same number of cycles and is typically near the end of its life too.

Replacing both at once costs more in parts and less in total, because the tension teardown and balancing labor happens once instead of twice. It also keeps the load even across the shaft, which protects the drums, bearings, and cables.

There are exceptions. A door with one spring installed recently and one much older may only need the old one. We tell you what we find rather than replacing on reflex.

Conversions And Upgrades

Some older doors still use extension springs stretched along the horizontal tracks. They work, but they are less forgiving when they fail, and safety cables are essential on that design.

Converting to a torsion system means new shaft, drums, cables, brackets, and springs, so it costs more than a like-for-like spring swap. What you get is smoother travel, better balance, and a system that fails more gracefully. Other upgrades often bundled with a spring visit include new bearings, nylon rollers, and replacement cables. Each is optional, priced separately, and explained before we start.

What Labor Covers

Torsion springs store a serious amount of energy. Releasing that safely takes proper winding bars, a supported door, and someone who does it regularly. That is what the labor line represents.

A complete visit includes measuring the existing setup, sizing the replacement, unwinding old tension, swapping the springs, checking drums and bearings, resetting cable tension evenly, winding to spec, testing the door by hand at multiple heights, then resetting opener force and travel and verifying the safety reverse.

Our spring replacement page walks through that sequence in more detail.

How To Compare Quotes Without Getting Burned

  • Ask for the wire size, length, inside diameter, and cycle rating being installed. Vague answers are a red flag.
  • Ask whether the quote covers one spring or both.
  • Ask what happens if the drums, bearings, or cables turn out to be worn.
  • Ask whether balancing and opener force adjustment are included or extra.
  • Ask what the warranty covers, in writing, and for how long.
  • Be skeptical of a very low phone price. It often becomes a much larger number once someone is in your garage.

We will not publish a single flat total here, because an honest number depends on your door. What we can promise is that the measurement comes first and the price is explained before any work starts.

Getting The Most Life From New Springs

Once the door is balanced, keep it that way. Lubricate the spring coils and roller bearings a few times a year, keep the hardware snug, and have the balance checked annually so the springs are not fighting a binding track. Our tune-up service covers that.

Also write the install date on the spring with a paint marker. In seven years, that one mark will tell you whether the door is due, and it turns a surprise failure into a planned appointment.

Spring Pricing Questions

Why will you not quote an exact price over the phone?

Because the spring has to match the door. Wire size, inside diameter, length, and wind direction all come from the actual door weight and drum setup. A number given before measuring is a guess, and guesses turn into surprises.

If one spring broke, should I replace both?

On a two-spring door, yes, in nearly every case. Both springs have the same cycle history, so the second one is typically close behind. Replacing the pair also keeps the balance even and avoids paying labor twice.

Are high-cycle springs worth the extra cost?

For heavy doors and busy households, often yes. A higher cycle rating costs more up front and is designed to last considerably longer under the same use. For a lightly used door, standard springs are usually the sensible choice.

Does an after-hours call cost more?

Yes. Appointments at 7:00 PM or later include a $100 after-hours service charge before any approved parts, repairs, conversions, or upgrades.
