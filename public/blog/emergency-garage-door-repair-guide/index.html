Emergency Garage Door Repair

Garage door emergencies come in a handful of shapes, and each one has a different first move. Find your scenario, secure the property, and gather the few details that get a technician to you prepared.

Scenario 1: Door Stuck Open And It Is Getting Dark

Treat this as a security problem first and a repair problem second. An open garage usually means access to tools, bikes, and often an unlocked door into the house.

Lock the interior door between the garage and the house. Move valuables out of view or into the house. Turn on exterior lighting. If a vehicle sits in the driveway, lock it and take the remote inside, since a remote in an unlocked car is a house key.

Do not try to lower a door that is stuck open if you suspect a broken spring. Disconnected from the opener, that door has nothing holding it up.

Scenario 2: Door Stuck Closed With The Car Inside

Check the low-stress options before anything else. Is there a side door or a walk-through? Can someone give you a ride for the next few hours? Removing the deadline usually removes the temptation to force the door.

If the door is closed and the springs are intact, an unpowered door can often be lifted by hand after pulling the release cord, with a helper and hands flat on the panel. If the door feels heavy, refuses to budge, or lifts unevenly, stop immediately. That is a broken spring or cable, and forcing it is how people get hurt.

Scenario 3: You Heard A Bang

A loud bang from the garage, sometimes mistaken for a gunshot, is the signature of a torsion spring letting go. Sometimes it is a cable.

Do not press the button to test it. Unplug the opener. Then look, from a safe distance, for a two-inch gap in the coiled spring above the door, or a cable hanging loose at either bottom corner. Photograph what you see and leave the door where it is.

Scenario 4: The Door Is Off Track Or Leaning

A door out of its rails carries its weight through whatever hardware is still attached. Unplug the opener, keep everyone out of the opening, and do not prop it with a ladder and then walk under it.

If the door leans into the driveway, put a cone or a trash can out so nobody walks into it after dark. Then book off-track repair, because realigning a door involves cable tension and track work.

Scenario 5: Vehicle Impact Or Storm Damage

After an impact, assume the door, the track, and the framing all moved. Check whether anyone is hurt, then unplug the opener and photograph the damage from several angles before anything is touched, which helps if an insurance claim follows.

Note whether the door is now blocking the only vehicle exit and whether the header or jamb looks displaced. Framing damage changes the repair from a door job to a door and structure job, and it is better to know that before a technician arrives.

The Six Things To Have Ready When You Call

  1. Your city and cross street, so routing is realistic.
  2. Whether the door is open, closed, or stopped part-way.
  3. What you heard or saw, in your own words, including the time it happened.
  4. Whether a vehicle is trapped and when you need it.
  5. Approximate door width, and whether it is steel, insulated, or wood, since weight drives parts.
  6. The opener brand and model from the label on the motor head.

Photos beat descriptions. A wide shot from inside, close shots of both bottom corners, one of the spring shaft, and one of the opener label give us enough to load the right parts.

Emergency Call Questions

What counts as a real garage door emergency?

A door stuck open overnight, a door stuck closed with a vehicle you need, a door off track or leaning, a broken spring or cable, or damage from a vehicle impact. Cosmetic dents and mild noise can wait for a normal appointment.

Is there an after-hours charge?

Appointments at 7:00 PM or later carry a $100 after-hours service charge, before any approved parts, repairs, conversions, or upgrades. We tell you that up front rather than at the door.

Can you come tonight?

Sometimes. It depends on technician availability, where the route already stands, the schedule, and whether the parts your door needs are on the truck. We will tell you honestly what is realistic when you call.

What if I cannot get my car out for work?

Say that first when you call. A trapped vehicle with a hard deadline is treated differently than a door that is simply noisy, and it changes how the day gets sequenced.
